# Apex PRD acceptance

The 85-page PDF and the two pasted specifications contain an early five-layout proposal and a later six-layout specification. The owner selected the final six-layout specification. Route dimensions in that specification are design targets. Measured centreline distances govern gameplay and the selector.

| PRD sections | Implementation and evidence | Acceptance or remaining qualification |
|---|---|---|
| 1–4 · Architecture, research, creative direction | Apex uses the existing Three.js engine, map catalog, game state machine and vehicle physics. Ten simulator references and the circuit references are linked in the research notes. | Implemented. Environmental resemblance to a commercial simulator remains an artistic assessment. |
| 5–8 · Layouts, terrain, sectors and crossover | Six configurations, nine signature sections, two physical decks at one crossing. A 5 m survey and full Track GT3 laps cover every configuration. | Tested. The measured routes differ from the initial length estimates. The actual elevation range is 145.5 m. |
| 9–10 · Track geometry, materials and banking | Asphalt, concrete slabs, serrated kerbs, sealed shoulders, gravel traps and grass. Roll and height affect wheel contacts. The banking survey reaches 24°. | Road/contact alignment and terrain coverage are sampled in the regression suite. The endurance test registers brief suspension unloading. |
| 11 · Stadium | Covered stands and open bleachers, individual seats, roof trusses, gallery, moving team flags, live circuit screen, timing pylon, countdown lights and grid markings. | Built in scene geometry. Spectator density follows the session and quality setting. |
| 12 · Pit operations | Forty numbered garages, individual boxes, tools, crew and animated service. Replacing tyres, fuel and repair updates player state. AI route through the pit on the four layouts that connect to it. | Tested both a legal pit-lane finish and a pit-lane shortcut attempt. Browser test drives the service UI. |
| 13–15 · Paddock, race control and circuit safety | Transporters, medical and media buildings, helipad, scrutineering, podium, hospitality, barriers, debris fence, tyre stacks, marshal huts and 16 electronic flag panels. Building perimeters and garage divisions have physical contacts. | Main facilities and lateral barriers represented. Service interiors, emergency dispatch, and every trackside access path have not been individually acceptance-tested. |
| 16–17 · Landscape and rendering | Mixed forest, wind in the canopy, fern beds, cut stone and exposed ridge outcrops, lake reeds, spectator deck, valley fields and farm buildings. Atmospheric scattering, material roughness, shadow maps and image-based lighting. | Verified using in-engine photographs from the ridge, stadium, pit lane and lakeside. |
| 18–19 · Conditions | Day, dawn, sunset, night and accelerated transitions. Clear, partly cloudy, overcast, mist, light and heavy rain, and drying conditions. Wet asphalt, spray, accumulated rubber, differing wet and dry grip. | Wet race and pit service verified. Heat and wetness are simplified models. Standing water is shaded without independent fluid dynamics. |
| 20 · Handling | Optional GT setup uses the project's GT tune, ABS, traction intervention, tyre temperature, wear, fuel and brake condition. Garage handling is restored after the event. | Six physics laps passed. ABS/TC indicators follow control intervention. |
| 21–23 · Racing, flags and opponents | Open practice, qualifying, sprint/endurance distances, linked weekend, time trial with saved ghost, track day and three-round championship standings. Six flag states, configurable limits, added time for repeated infringements, difficulty and wet-line AI. Vehicle contact and avoidance use simplified car footprints. | Browser test advances all weekend stages, seeds the race grid from qualifying and reloads a PB ghost. Missed gates, invalid laps, a wide rejoin, flag logic and penalties have regression coverage. Opponents follow a steering path and have a simpler physical model than the player. |
| 24 · Attractions | Academy slalom and braking-box exercise, Heritage Gallery exterior, podium, upper hospitality level and scenic cameras. | Academy gates are interactive and scored. Kart racing, a walk-through museum and a separate gymkhana arena are optional ideas. |
| 25–26 · Weekend activity and sound | Practice, qualifying and race crowd densities, crews, flags, lights and live boards. Procedural engines, banking/tyre feedback, rain, forest birds, foliage, crowd and pit ambience. Stereo rival pass-bys, pit/underpass reverberation and optional race-control voice. | Voice announcements depend on availability of an English local browser voice. Messages work independently. |
| 27–29 · Original assets and materials | Original generated artwork, texture treatment, procedural landscape, Blender-authored fictional grid coupe and timing pylon. | Shipped Apex-specific file manifest and attribution are supplied. Many buildings share instanced procedural meshes. |
| 30–31 · Cameras, replays and interface | Countdown camera, aerial views, section cameras, podium and pit views, full-field session replay with scrubber, depth of field, lens, exposure, clean photo output and hideable interface. Selector includes plan and elevation, distances, rules and conditions. | Chrome exercised full-field replay and the optical post-process. The replay is kept in memory for the current session. |
| 32 · Browser performance | Four graphics tiers, spatial culling and LOD. Adaptive race resolution is selectable. The browser report records actual quality settings, GPU, render sizes, loading stages and frame-time percentiles. | Desktop measured and the setup flow exercised in an 844 × 390 browser viewport. Mobile hardware, every browser, and sustained 60 FPS under all conditions require separate evidence. |
| 33–36 · Integration, testing and delivery | Source, assets, ownership records, engineering survey, route data, screenshots from all named sections, build output, tests and hardware measurements. | See the delivery index and raw outputs. Human competitive balance and approval of the visual quality remain open. |
